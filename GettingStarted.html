About this guide

This guide is for anyone new to Clipman, and especially for people who use a screen reader such as JAWS, NVDA or Narrator on Windows, VoiceOver on Mac, iPhone and iPad, or TalkBack on Android. It does not replace the full Clipman manual. Instead, it walks you through the parts most people need, one task at a time, with worked examples you can follow along with at your own pace.

Some things to know before you begin:

  • You do not need to read it all. Parts 1 and 2 cover everyday use, and they are where you will get the most benefit for your time. Everything after that is optional, so come back to it whenever you are ready.
  • Every tutorial stands on its own. If a tutorial relies on something from an earlier one, it links back to it, so you can jump straight to the one you need.
  • Headings are your friend. Each part is a level 2 heading and each tutorial is a level 3 heading, so you can move through the guide using your screen reader's heading navigation.
  • Keys are given for each platform. Most desktop commands use Ctrl on Windows and Linux and Command on Mac. Where the keys differ, both are given, Windows first. Linux uses the same keys as Windows unless the guide says otherwise.
  • Symbol keys are spelled out. So that your screen reader always reads them, keys such as the backslash are written as words, for example Ctrl+Alt+Backslash. Grave means the grave accent key, which on most keyboards is just below Escape, to the left of the 1 key.
  • Screen reader commands are not included. Where a step depends on your screen reader, such as reading the status bar, the guide says so and leaves the keystroke to you, because it differs between screen readers.
  • Names of buttons and options are in quotation marks. When the guide mentions something you will find on screen, such as the option “Run Clipman at Windows startup”, its name is in quotation marks, exactly as your screen reader will say it.
  • Press F1 when you are unsure. On Windows, Mac and Linux, pressing F1 on any Clipman control explains what that control does. Clipman keeps these explanations out of the way, so your screen reader only reads a control's name and value as you move around. Press F1 whenever you want the longer explanation, then Escape to go back.

This guide was written for Clipman 3.1.11 on Windows, 3.1.12 on Mac, 3.1.9 on Linux, 3.1.7 on iPhone and iPad, and 3.1.4 on Android. Later versions may add features or change some wording.

Why use Clipman?

Think about how often you copy and paste in a day. An email address from one message into another. A reference number from a letter into a web form. A paragraph from a report into an email. Your computer's clipboard holds only one thing at a time, so when you need three pieces of information, you make the same trip three times: switch to the source, find your place, copy, switch back, find your place again, paste. With a screen reader, every one of those switches means listening your way back to where you were. Each trip is small, but over a working day they add up to a lot of time and a lot of tedium.

Clipman changes the way you work with information. Because it remembers what you copy, you can go through a document once, copying everything you need as you go, then move to where it all belongs and paste each piece, or all of them in one go. Things you type again and again, such as your address, a standard reply or a reference number, become two or three keystrokes away, from any app. Your clipboard stops being a single slot that is constantly overwritten, and becomes a collection you can search, name, organize and come back to.

The benefits grow when you use more than one device. Without Clipman, getting a link or a block of text from your phone to your computer usually means emailing or messaging it to yourself, opening that message on the other device, and copying it out again. With shared history, you add it to Clipman on one device and it is waiting for you on the other.

Clipman is designed around the keyboard and the screen reader, not adapted for them afterwards. Its lists are quick to move through, its controls are properly labeled, and its short sounds tell you what has happened without interrupting your speech.

Like any tool worth having, it takes a little time to learn. You do not need to learn it all at once. Work through Tutorials 1 to 4 in Part 2, and you will already be saving time every day. The rest of this guide will be here when you want to go further.

What Clipman does

When you copy something, your computer or phone puts it on the clipboard. The clipboard only holds one thing at a time, so when you copy something new, the old item is gone.

Clipman quietly keeps a list of the things you copy. That list is called your history. Later, you can open the history, pick something you copied an hour ago or last week, and put it back on the clipboard so you can paste it again.

On top of that, Clipman can:

  • keep important entries at the top of the list, called “pinning”, so they are always one or two keys away;
  • give entries short names, so you hear “Home address” instead of the whole address as you go through your history;
  • paste a saved entry into any app with a single shortcut, without opening Clipman;
  • keep your history in step between your computer, phone and tablet;
  • stay out of the way when you copy passwords and other private information.

On Windows and Mac, Clipman runs quietly in the background and records what you copy automatically. On iPhone, iPad and Android, the operating system does not let apps watch the clipboard in the background, so you add things to Clipman yourself. Part 6 explains how.

Words you will meet

Clipman uses some words in particular ways. You may wish to learn these now, which will help everything make more sense. Come back here whenever a word in a tutorial is unfamiliar. Each tutorial also explains its terms the first time they come up.

The basics

Clipboard
The place your device keeps the last thing you copied or cut. Pasting puts it into whatever you are working on.
History
Clipman's list of things you have copied.
Entry
One item in your history. If you copy a sentence, that sentence becomes an entry. The full manual sometimes calls an entry a “clip”.
Monitoring
Whether Clipman is currently recording what you copy. When monitoring is on, new copies are added to history. When it is off, Clipman ignores them. You can turn monitoring on and off with a shortcut.
Sections
The history window is divided into tabs called sections. The Text section is always there. Links and Rich Text sections appear only if you turn them on. The File History section is always there on Windows, Mac and Linux. Optional Links and Rich Text sections are just filtered views of the same history, not separate lists.
Status bar
A line at the bottom of the history window that tells you how many entries the current section holds and whether Clipman is ready, for example “262 clipboard entries. Ready.” Use your screen reader's command for reading the status bar to hear it.
Global hotkey
A keyboard shortcut that works from any app, not just from inside Clipman. Opening the history window, Ctrl+Alt+Backslash on Windows and Linux or Option+Shift+Grave on Mac, is the most common one.

Organizing entries

Pinned entry
An entry you have fixed to the top of the list. Pinned entries are never removed by automatic cleanup and the first ten can be copied back into the clipboard with Ctrl+1 to Ctrl+0 when the Clipman window is open.
Normal entries
Everything that is not pinned. A row reading “Normal entries” separates pinned entries from normal ones.
Name
An optional short label for an entry, such as “Work phone”. Clipman reads the name instead of the full text, which makes long entries much quicker to recognize. The name is never pasted. Only the entry's text is.
Group
A folder-like label you can give several entries, such as “Email replies”. You can then show only that group.
Filter
The list box at the top of the history window that chooses what to show: all entries, only pinned ones, only named ones, one group, or entries from one device. You reach it with Alt+G (Mac: Option+G).
Device
The computer, phone or tablet that added an entry. This matters once you share history between several devices.
Manual order
An order you arrange yourself by moving entries up and down, instead of sorting by date or text.

Features

Quick Clip
A small editor for typing a new entry straight into Clipman, without copying it from somewhere else first.
Quick Paste
A way to paste one particular saved entry into any app using its own keyboard shortcut, without opening Clipman.
Quick Paste target
An entry you have set up for Quick Paste. In Entry Properties, it is the entry with the option “Use as quick-paste target” checked. Once checked, you give the entry its own Quick Paste hotkey.
Quick Paste mode
What happens to your clipboard when a Quick Paste hotkey is used. Tutorial 9 explains the three choices.
Template
An entry containing placeholders, such as {{year_full}}, that Clipman fills in at the moment you paste it. Good for date stamps.
Entry Properties
The window you open with F2 to change an entry's name, group, text, pinned state, template setting and Quick Paste hotkey.
ClipMerge
An optional feature that builds one clipboard item from several separate copies. You copy normally, then copy each extra piece twice quickly to add it on.
Push to other devices
Sending an existing entry to your other devices as if you had just copied it, so they can put it on their clipboards.
Secrets
A separate, password-protected list for short private things like a PIN or a system password. Secrets never appear in normal history and are never shared with other devices.
File History event
A record of files or folders you copied or cut in File Explorer or Finder. Clipman stores where the files are, not the files themselves. These records are called events rather than entries, and they stay on the device that recorded them.

Privacy

Ignored application
An app whose copies Clipman never records, such as a password manager.
Sensitive data exclusion
An optional filter that stops Clipman recording text that looks like a card number, a long security token or similar.
Privacy signal
A hidden marker some apps, mainly password managers, attach to what they copy to say “do not record this”. Clipman on Windows respects these automatically.
History password
An optional password that encrypts your history file so nobody can read it without the password. It is required for some sharing methods.

Sharing history between devices

Storage type, or storage mode
Where your history lives. Windows calls this the storage type and the mobile apps call it the storage mode. There are three choices, described next.
Local
History stays on one device only. On Windows and Mac this is the “Local or shared folder” choice with Clipman's default folder.
Shared folder
History lives in a folder that a service such as Dropbox, OneDrive or iCloud Drive keeps in step between your devices.
Clipman Server
A separate program you run yourself, on a home computer, a Raspberry Pi or a rented server, that keeps history in step between devices.
Data folder
The folder on your computer where Clipman keeps your history file, called clipman-history.clipdb, along with that computer's settings.
Sync, synchronize
Keeping the same history on several devices, so an entry added on one appears on the others.
Connection file
A file ending in .clpconf that holds a Clipman Server's address and access token, so you can set up a device without typing them. Treat it like a password.
Sync rules, channels and core
An advanced way to split shared history into separate parts, called channels, so each device only downloads the parts it wants. The main, always-shared part is called core. Most people never need this.

Part 1: Installing Clipman

Find your platform below. When you have finished, go on to Part 2 if you use Windows, Mac or Linux, or to Part 6 if you use an iPhone, iPad or Android device.

Windows

The one shortcut to remember: once Clipman is running, press Ctrl+Alt+Backslash from any app to open your history. Press it again, or press Escape, to hide it. Almost everything in this guide starts with that shortcut.

Clipman for Windows is portable. That means there is no installer: you unzip it into a folder and run it from there. Clipman keeps its settings and history in a folder called Settings next to the program, so choose a folder you own.

  1. Go to the Clipman releases page and download the Windows ZIP file. It is named like Clipman-3.1.11.zip. You can ignore files with Android, Mac, Linux, Server or CLI in their names.
  2. Create a folder for Clipman in your user area, for example C:\Users\YourName\Clipman. Avoid C:\Program Files, because Clipman needs to write its settings next to itself and Windows protects that folder.
  3. Extract the ZIP into the new folder. In File Explorer, focus the ZIP file, press the Applications key or Shift+F10, choose “Extract All”, enter your folder, and press Enter.
  4. Open the folder and run clipman.exe. You will hear a short sound. That is the “monitoring on” sound, and it means Clipman is running and recording what you copy.
  5. Try it now: press Ctrl+Alt+Backslash. The history window opens. It will be nearly empty for now. Press Escape to hide it again.

Clipman has no main window of its own. It lives in the notification area, sometimes called the system tray. To reach it, press Windows+B, use the arrow keys to find Clipman (you may need to open the hidden icons first by pressing Enter on the button that shows them), then press the Applications key for its menu. The menu lets you show history, turn monitoring on and off, open Secrets, open Preferences, and exit Clipman.

To start Clipman automatically when you sign in:

  1. Open Preferences. From the notification area menu choose “Preferences”, or, with the history window open, press Ctrl+Comma.
  2. Press Ctrl+5 to go to the “Startup and updates” tab.
  3. Tab to the option “Run Clipman at Windows startup” and press Space to check it.
  4. Press Escape or activate the “Close” button.

Good to know: Windows Preferences has no Save button. Your changes are saved when you close the window. The six tabs can be reached directly with Ctrl+1 to Ctrl+6: General, File history, Hotkeys, Storage and Password, Startup and updates, and Sensitive data.

Mac

The one shortcut to remember: once Clipman is running, press Option+Shift+Grave from any app to open your history. Press it again, or press Escape, to hide it.

  1. Go to the Clipman releases page and download the Mac ZIP that matches your Mac. There is one for Apple Silicon (M1 and later) and one for Intel. If you are not sure which you have, open the Apple menu, choose “About This Mac”, and check the Chip or Processor line.
  2. Open the downloaded ZIP to unzip it. You will get Clipman.app.
  3. Move Clipman.app into your Applications folder. In Finder, select it, press Command+C, open Applications with Command+Shift+A, and press Command+V.
  4. Open Clipman from Applications. You will hear the “monitoring on” sound.
  5. Try it now: press Option+Shift+Grave. The history window opens. Press Escape to hide it again.

On Mac, Clipman lives in the menu bar as a menu extra, like the Wi-Fi or volume menus. Use VoiceOver's method for reaching menu extras to find it. When a Clipman window is open, Clipman also has a normal menu bar with File, Edit, Actions, Groups, Quick Paste, View, Window and Help menus.

To start Clipman when you log in, open Preferences with Command+Comma, check the option “Run Clipman at login”, and activate the “Save and Close” button (or press Command+S).

The first time Clipman tries to paste for you, macOS may ask you to allow Clipman to control your computer in Privacy and Security settings. Allow it, then quit and reopen Clipman.

iPhone and iPad

There is no shortcut to remember on iPhone and iPad. You open Clipman like any other app, and it opens straight to your history.

  1. Install Clipman from the App Store.
  2. So that Clipman can read what you copy without asking every time, open the iOS Settings app, go to “Apps”, then “Clipman”, then “Paste from Other Apps”, and choose “Allow”. If you prefer to be asked each time, choose “Ask”.
  3. Open Clipman. To check or change where history is kept, activate the “More” button at the top right, choose “Settings”, and look at “Storage mode”. Choose “Local” if you only use Clipman on this device for now. You can change this later.
  4. Activate the “Save” button at the top of Settings.

Then read Part 6 to learn how adding and copying entries works on iPhone and iPad.

Android

As on iPhone, there is no shortcut to remember. You open Clipman from your apps and it opens straight to your history.

Clipman for Android is currently a test build, offered as an APK file rather than through the Play Store. It needs Android 8 or newer.

  1. Download the Android APK from the Clipman releases page.
  2. Open the APK from your Downloads or Files app. If Android asks whether to allow installing apps from that source, allow it for this install.
  3. Install and open Clipman. Settings opens the first time. Choose “Local” under history storage for now, then activate the “Save” button at the top.

Linux

On Linux, the default shortcut for opening history is the same as on Windows: Ctrl+Alt+Backslash.

Clipman for Linux works with Clipman Server, so you need a server set up first (see Clipman Server, in brief). The Linux download includes an installer and its own manual, which covers screen reader setup, the differences between X11 and Wayland, and Linux shortcuts. Once installed, the keys in this guide for Windows also apply to Linux unless the Linux manual says otherwise.

The sounds Clipman makes

Clipman uses short sounds to tell you what happened without interrupting your screen reader. They are the same on every platform, and once you know them you will rarely need to check what Clipman did. You can turn them off in Preferences (Windows: the option “Play sounds” on the “General” tab).

  • Monitoring on and monitoring off: played when Clipman starts and when you toggle monitoring.
  • Copy: played when something is saved to history, or when you copy an entry from Clipman.
  • Skip: played when you copy something while monitoring is off, so you know it was not saved. Also played when an action fails.
  • Exclude: played when Clipman deliberately refuses to save something because it looks private.
  • Remote: played when text from another of your devices is placed on this device's clipboard.
  • Merge: played when ClipMerge combines two copies.

If you want different sounds, the full manual's Sounds section explains how to replace them with your own WAV files.

Part 2: Your first steps (Windows, Mac and Linux)

These eight tutorials cover everything most people use day to day. Do them in order the first time. Each one only takes a few minutes, and each one builds a habit that will save you time.

You will use one shortcut in every tutorial: the one that opens the history window.

  • Windows and Linux: Ctrl+Alt+Backslash
  • Mac: Option+Shift+Grave

Pressing it again, or pressing Escape, hides the window. Hiding the window does not quit Clipman. It keeps running in the background. If the shortcut does nothing on your keyboard, see The history shortcut does nothing in Troubleshooting.

Tutorial 1: Paste something you copied earlier

What you will learn: how Clipman records what you copy, and how to get an older item back.

Worked example: you copy three short lines, then paste the first one again even though you have copied two other things since.

  1. Open a plain text editor, such as Notepad on Windows or TextEdit on Mac.
  2. Type these three lines, pressing Enter after each:
    • Apples
    • Bananas
    • Cherries
  3. Go to the first line. Select it with Shift+End and copy it with Ctrl+C (Mac: Command+C). You hear the copy sound. “Apples” is now in your history.
  4. Do the same for “Bananas” and then “Cherries”. The clipboard now holds only “Cherries”, but Clipman has kept all three.
  5. Move to the end of the document and press Enter to start a new line.
  6. Open the history window with Ctrl+Alt+Backslash (Mac: Option+Shift+Grave). Focus lands on the Text history list, with the newest entry, “Cherries”, at the top.
  7. Press Down Arrow twice to reach “Apples”.
  8. Press Enter. Clipman puts “Apples” on the clipboard and closes the history window, returning you to your editor.
  9. Press Ctrl+V (Mac: Command+V). “Apples” is pasted.

What just happened: pressing Enter on an entry copies it and closes Clipman. You then paste as you normally would. By default, the entry you used moves back to the top of the list, so the things you use often stay near the top.

Variations:

  • To copy an entry but keep the history window open, press Ctrl+C (Mac: Command+C) instead of Enter.
  • To save yourself the paste step, turn on the option “After Enter, paste into the previous application” on the “General” tab of Preferences. Then pressing Enter in history pastes straight into the app you came from.

Tutorial 2: Select and paste several entries at once

What you will learn: how to choose several entries from your history and paste them all together with a single paste. This is one of the biggest time savers in Clipman. Instead of going back and forth between two documents, you copy everything you need in one pass, then paste it all in one go.

New terms: selecting an entry marks it to be included. You can select entries that sit next to each other in the list, or pick out entries from different places in the list.

Worked example: a colleague needs a customer's name, phone number and email address, which are in a letter you have open. You copy all three from the letter, then paste them into an email to your colleague in one go.

  1. In the letter, copy the customer's name. Then copy their phone number. Then copy their email address. You hear the copy sound each time. There is no need to switch to your email in between.
  2. Switch to a new email to your colleague and move to where you want the details to go.
  3. Open the history window. The email address is at the top, the phone number is second and the name is third.
  4. Select all three. The three entries are next to each other, so press Shift+Down Arrow twice. Your screen reader announces each entry as it is added to the selection.
  5. Press Enter. Clipman copies all three entries to the clipboard as one item and closes the history window.
  6. Paste with Ctrl+V (Mac: Command+V). The three details are pasted together, each one separated from the next by a blank line.

Picking entries from different places in the list (Windows)

Often the entries you want are not next to each other. Perhaps you copied something else in between. On Windows you can pick entries out one by one:

  1. Move to the first entry you want. It is selected as you arrive on it.
  2. Hold down Ctrl and use the arrow keys to move to the next entry you want. While you hold Ctrl, moving does not change what is selected.
  3. Still holding Ctrl, press Space. The entry is added to the selection.
  4. Repeat for each entry you want. If you add one by mistake, press Ctrl+Space on it again to remove it from the selection.
  5. Let go of Ctrl and press Enter to copy them all.

Be careful not to press an arrow key without Ctrl held down, as that selects only the entry you move to and clears the rest.

On Mac, use Shift with the arrow keys to select entries next to each other, or Command+A to select every entry shown. On Linux, use Shift with the arrow keys, or Ctrl+A to select every entry shown. Selecting every entry shown is especially handy together with a group filter (see Tutorial 6).

Good to know:

  • Entries are always pasted in the order they appear in the list, from top to bottom, not in the order you selected them. If you want a different order, switch to manual order and move entries with Alt+Up Arrow and Alt+Down Arrow (Mac: Option+Up Arrow and Option+Down Arrow) first.
  • By default, each entry is separated from the next by a blank line. You can choose a single new line, a space, a comma and space, nothing at all, or your own separator, with the setting “Selected-clips join” on the “General” tab of Preferences.
  • Many other commands work on a selection too, including pinning, grouping, deleting and the cleanup actions in Tutorial 11.

Tutorial 3: Find an entry quickly

What you will learn: three ways to find an entry without arrowing through a long list.

Once your history has a few hundred entries, arrowing is slow. Try these instead, with the history window open.

First-letter navigation

Type the first letter of an entry and Clipman jumps to the next entry that starts with that letter. You can type the first few letters to be more precise. Using the list from Tutorial 1, typing B A jumps to “Bananas”. Type the letters quickly, one straight after the other. If you pause for more than about a second, Clipman starts a new search. Pause, then press the same letter again to move on to the next entry starting with that letter.

This only matches the beginning of an entry. Typing a word from the middle of an entry will not find it. For that, use search, below.

Search anywhere in an entry

  1. Press Ctrl+F (Mac: Command+F).
  2. Type a word or part of a word that appears anywhere in the entry, such as “nan”, and press Enter. Clipman moves to the first match.
  3. Press F3 for the next match, or Shift+F3 for the previous one.

Search also looks in entry names, which you will learn about in Tutorial 5.

Move in big steps

  • Home and End go to the first and last entries.
  • Page Up and Page Down move one screenful at a time.

Tutorial 4: Keep your favorites at the top

What you will learn: how to pin entries, and how to copy a pinned entry with a single shortcut.

New terms: a pinned entry stays at the top of the list, above everything else, and is never removed by automatic cleanup. Everything else is a normal entry.

Worked example: you often type your email address, so you pin it.

  1. Type your email address into any text field, select it, and copy it.
  2. Open the history window. Your email address is the top entry.
  3. Press Shift+Enter. The entry is pinned.
  4. Press Home and listen. The entry now begins with the number 1, which means it is the first pinned entry. Below the pinned entries you will find a row reading “Normal entries”. That row is only a divider and cannot be copied.
  5. Press Escape to hide the window.

Now, whenever you want your email address:

  1. Open the history window with Ctrl+Alt+Backslash (Mac: Option+Shift+Grave).
  2. Press Ctrl+1. Clipman copies the first pinned entry and closes.
  3. Paste it.

The first ten pinned entries are numbered 1 to 9 and then 0, and each can be copied by pressing Control and its number, on both Windows and Mac.

Tips:

  • Press Backspace in the list to jump past your pinned entries to the first normal entry.
  • To change the order of pinned entries, select one and press Alt+Up Arrow or Alt+Down Arrow (Mac: Option+Up Arrow or Option+Down Arrow).
  • To unpin, select the entry and press Shift+Enter again.
  • Pinned entries cannot be deleted by accident. Unpin an entry first if you want to delete it.

Tutorial 5: Give an entry a name

What you will learn: how to label an entry so it is easy to recognize and find.

New terms: a name is a short label for an entry. Clipman reads the name first, which saves you listening to a long address or a web link every time you pass it. The name is never pasted. Only the entry's own text is.

Worked example: you save your home address and name it “Home address”.

  1. Type your address on a few lines in your text editor, select it all, and copy it.
  2. Open the history window. Your address is the top entry.
  3. Press F2. The Entry Properties window opens.
  4. Focus starts in the “Name” field. Type Home address.
  5. If you also want to pin it, tab to the “Pinned” checkbox and press Space.
  6. Save your changes. Windows and Linux: press Ctrl+Enter, or Alt+S also works on Windows. Mac: press Command+Enter or Command+S.

The entry is now read as “Home address” followed by its text. When you copy it, only the address itself is copied.

Finding a named entry: in the history list, type a forward slash followed by the start of the name, for example /Home, and Clipman jumps to it.

Good to know: sometimes you do want the name and the text together, for example “Home address” on one line and the address below it. Select the entry and press Ctrl+Enter in the history list (Mac: Command+Enter). This is called Copy Name and Content.

Tutorial 6: Sort entries into groups

What you will learn: how to put related entries into a group, and how to show only that group.

New terms: a group is a label shared by several entries. The filter is the list box at the top of the history window that chooses which entries to show.

Clipman already groups new entries automatically by the app you copied them from, so you may find groups named after your browser, email program or word processor. If you would rather group everything yourself, turn off the option “Automatically group new clips by source application” on the “General” tab of Preferences.

Worked example: you collect a few standard email replies into a group called “Replies”.

  1. Open the history window and select an entry you want in the group. To select several, use the methods in Tutorial 2.
  2. Press Ctrl+G (Mac: Command+G).
  3. Type Replies and press Enter. If the group already exists, you can choose it from the list instead.
  4. To show only that group, press Alt+G (Mac: Option+G) to move to the filter.
    • Windows: use the arrow keys to choose “Replies”, then press Enter. Focus returns to the list, now showing only that group.
    • Mac: choose “Replies” from the filter menu.
  5. To see everything again, return to the filter and choose “All”.

Two ways to choose what is shown

There are two places to choose a group, and they do exactly the same job. Use whichever suits you.

  • The history filter is a list box at the top of the history window. Press Alt+G (Mac: Option+G) to reach it. It is the quickest way once you are used to it.
  • The Groups menu is in the menu bar. On Windows, press Alt, then P. On Mac, it is the Groups menu in the menu bar. It lists the same choices as menu items, and a “Devices” submenu for showing entries from one device. On Windows, each choice is read with its shortcut, so it is a good way to hear all your choices and learn their shortcuts.

Shortcut: the first ten choices can be selected directly with Alt+1 to Alt+0 (Mac: Option+1 to Option+0), without going to the filter or the menu. They are always in this order: All, Pinned, Named, Ungrouped, then your own groups. So Alt+1 always shows everything, Alt+2 shows pinned entries and Alt+3 shows named entries.

Good to know: group names ignore capital letters, so “Replies” and “replies” are the same group. To remove an entry from its group, press Ctrl+G and choose “No group”, or leave the name blank.

Tutorial 7: Review, edit and delete entries

What you will learn: how to read a long entry comfortably, fix its text, and remove entries you no longer want.

Read an entry in full

Select an entry and press F4. A read-only window opens with the entry's full text, which you can read line by line with your arrow keys. Press Tab to reach a Details list showing when the entry was added and last used, which device added it, and how long it is. Press Escape to close.

Fix an entry's text

Worked example: you copied a phone number with extra words around it, “Call me on 01234 567890 thanks”, and you want only the number.

  1. Select the entry and press F2.
  2. Move to the “Clipboard text” field, which comes after the Name, Group and Quick Paste controls. Press Tab until you reach it, or on Windows press Alt+C. This is the text that will be pasted.
  3. Edit it as you would in any text field. Delete everything except 01234 567890. In this field, Enter adds a new line rather than saving.
  4. Save with Ctrl+Enter (Mac: Command+Enter).

Tip: in this field and in the F4 window, Ctrl+Left Arrow and Ctrl+Right Arrow (Mac: Option+Left Arrow and Option+Right Arrow) stop at slashes, dots and other punctuation. That makes it much easier to move around inside long web links and file paths.

Delete entries

  1. Select one or more entries.
  2. Press Delete (Mac: Command+Backspace).
  3. Clipman asks you to confirm. Choose “Yes”. The dialog also lets you stop being asked in future.

Pinned entries are skipped when you delete. Unpin them first if you really want them gone.

Let Clipman tidy up for you

On the “General” tab of Preferences, the settings “Maximum entries” and “Maximum entry age, days” let Clipman remove old entries automatically. Use 0 for no limit. Pinned and named entries are always kept, so Clipman lets you keep important information at your fingertips while getting rid of unnecessary clutter.

Tutorial 8: Save text without copying it

What you will learn: how to type a new entry straight into Clipman.

New terms: Quick Clip is a small editor for creating an entry from scratch. It does not touch your clipboard, so whatever you copied last stays there.

Worked example: you want a ready-made reply saying you are out of the office.

  1. Open the history window and press Ctrl+N (Mac: Command+N). The Quick Clip window opens with focus in the “Clipboard text” field.
  2. Type: Thanks for your message. I am away until Monday and will reply when I am back.
  3. Move to the “Name” field, which is near the top of the window. Press Shift+Tab until you reach it, or on Windows press Alt+N. Type Out of office.
  4. Tab to the “Group” field and type Replies, or leave it blank.
  5. Tab to the “Pinned” checkbox and press Space if you want it at the top.
  6. Save with Ctrl+Enter (Mac: Command+Enter).

The order of fields is the same in Quick Clip and in Entry Properties: Name, Group, the Pinned and Template checkboxes, the Quick Paste settings, and then Clipboard text, followed by the buttons. On Windows, each labeled field has an Alt shortcut: Alt+N for Name, Alt+G for Group, Alt+P for Pinned and Alt+C for Clipboard text.

Tip: you can open Quick Clip from any app without opening history first. On the “Hotkeys” tab of Preferences (Ctrl+3 on Windows), focus the “Quick Clip hotkey” field and press the shortcut you want, such as Ctrl+Win+F8. The tray menu also has a “New Quick Clip” command.

If you get interrupted: on Windows and Mac, the Quick Clip hotkey also puts an unfinished Quick Clip aside. Press it once to hide the editor and go back to what you were doing. Press it again, or open the history window, and your unfinished text is still there. There is only ever one unfinished Quick Clip, so pressing the hotkey again never opens a second, empty editor. The draft is kept until you save it, cancel it with Escape, or quit Clipman. Quitting Clipman throws it away, so save it first.

Tutorial 9: Paste a saved entry from anywhere with Quick Paste

What you will learn: how to give an entry its own keyboard shortcut, so you can paste it into any app without opening Clipman at all.

New terms:

  • Quick Paste means pasting a saved entry with its own global hotkey.
  • A Quick Paste target is simply an entry that has been set up this way. In Entry Properties, it is the entry with the option “Use as quick-paste target” checked.
  • The Quick Paste hotkey is the shortcut that pastes it. Each target has its own.
  • The Quick Paste mode decides what happens to your clipboard afterwards.

Worked example: you want your email signature pasted whenever you press Ctrl+Win+F9.

  1. Create the entry. Either copy your signature from an email, or open history and use Quick Clip (Ctrl+N, see Tutorial 8). Give it the name Signature so you can recognize it later.
  2. In the history window, select the Signature entry and press F2 to open Entry Properties.
  3. Check the option “Use as quick-paste target”. On Windows you can press Alt+U.
  4. Move to the “Quick Paste hotkey” field (Windows: Alt+H). This field records the next key combination you press instead of typing letters. Press Ctrl+Win+F9. Your screen reader should announce the new shortcut. To clear the field and start again, press Delete or Backspace.
  5. Leave the “Quick Paste mode” setting on its default, “Paste and restore previous clipboard”, for now.
  6. Save with Ctrl+Enter (Mac: Command+Enter), then close the history window.
  7. Open a new email, move to the end of the message, and press Ctrl+Win+F9. Your signature is pasted.

The three Quick Paste modes

  • “Paste and restore previous clipboard” (the default): pastes the entry, then puts back whatever was on your clipboard before. Your clipboard is left exactly as it was. This is the right choice most of the time.
  • “Paste and keep target on clipboard”: pastes the entry and leaves it on the clipboard, so you can paste it again with the normal paste command.
  • “Copy to clipboard only”: does not paste anything. It only puts the entry on the clipboard, ready for you to paste yourself. This is useful for apps where automatic pasting does not work well.

Choosing a good shortcut

  • Use two modifier keys, such as Ctrl+Win, Ctrl+Shift or Ctrl+Alt, plus a function key or letter. Function keys are the least likely to clash with other programs or with your screen reader.
  • Bear in mind that if you use Leasey, an add-on for JAWS, many Ctrl+Shift and function key combinations may already be in use, and the Leasey shortcuts will likely take precedence. That is why this guide suggests Ctrl+Win with a function key. Avoid Ctrl+Win+F4, which Windows uses to close a virtual desktop.
  • On Mac, avoid Control+Option combinations, because VoiceOver uses those keys.
  • Clipman refuses shortcuts that would break normal typing, such as Ctrl+C or a single modifier with a letter. If you choose a shortcut with only one modifier, such as Ctrl+F9, Clipman warns you first.
  • On many non-US keyboards, Ctrl+Alt together act like the AltGr key that types accented letters and symbols. If a Ctrl+Alt shortcut types a character instead of pasting, use the Alt key to the left of the spacebar, or choose a different combination.

Reviewing your Quick Paste targets

Open the “Quick Paste” menu in the history window (Windows: press Alt, then move to “Quick Paste”). It lists every entry with a Quick Paste hotkey. Choosing one moves focus to that entry, ready for F2 if you want to change or remove its shortcut. Entries with a Quick Paste hotkey also announce the shortcut and mode as part of their row, so you always know which entries have one.

Good to know: Quick Paste works even while monitoring is off. Quick Paste hotkeys belong to each computer, so if you share history with another computer, it keeps its own set of shortcuts.

Tutorial 10: Insert today's date with a template

What you will learn: how to make an entry that fills in the date, time or other details at the moment you paste it.

New terms: a template is an entry containing placeholders in double curly brackets, such as {{year_full}}. When you copy or Quick Paste a template, Clipman replaces each placeholder with its current value. The stored entry itself never changes, so it is ready to use again next time.

Worked example: a date line for the top of letters, such as “16 October 2026”.

  1. Open the history window and press Ctrl+N (Mac: Command+N) for Quick Clip.
  2. In the “Clipboard text” field, type exactly: {{day_of_month}} {{month_name_full}} {{year_full}}
  3. Go to the “Name” field and type Today's date.
  4. Check the option “Template entry” (Windows: Alt+T).
  5. Check the result with the “Preview template” button. Clipman shows the text as it would be pasted today.
  6. Save with Ctrl+Enter (Mac: Command+Enter).

Each time you copy this entry, you get that day's date. To make it even faster, give it a Quick Paste hotkey as in Tutorial 9.

Some useful placeholders:

  • {{day_name_full}}: the day of the week, such as Friday.
  • {{day_of_month}}: the day number, such as 16. Use {{day_of_month_padded}} for 05 instead of 5.
  • {{month_name_full}}: the month name, such as October. {{month_num_padded}} gives 10.
  • {{year_full}}: the year, such as 2026.
  • {{hour_24_padded}}:{{minute_padded}}: the time, such as 14:05.
  • {{username}} and {{os_name}}: your sign-in name and your operating system.

You do not need to remember these. In Entry Properties and Quick Clip, the “Insert sample” button offers ready-made templates and the “Insert field” button inserts any placeholder at the cursor. The full manual lists them all.

Tutorial 11: Clean up messy text

What you will learn: how to fix common problems with copied text without opening another program.

Worked example: you copied a paragraph from a PDF and it arrived broken into short lines. You want it as one line.

  1. Copy the paragraph from the PDF as usual.
  2. Open the history window. The paragraph is the top entry.
  3. Open the “Actions” menu (Windows: Alt+A) and choose “Convert to single line”. On Windows the shortcut is Ctrl+Shift+L.
  4. The entry is changed in history and the fixed version is placed on your clipboard, ready to paste.

Other actions you may find useful (shortcuts are for Windows):

  • “Trim whitespace” (Ctrl+Shift+T): removes spaces and blank lines from the start and end.
  • “Remove blank lines” (Ctrl+Shift+B).
  • “Copy as plain text” (Ctrl+Shift+C): copies without any formatting, and does not change the entry.
  • “HTML to readable text” (Ctrl+Shift+H): turns web page source code into the words you would read on the page.
  • “Remove URL tracking” (Ctrl+Shift+R, Mac: Command+Shift+R) and “Clean link for sharing” (Ctrl+Shift+S, Mac: Command+Shift+S): see Working with links.
  • Uppercase, lowercase, URL encode and decode, HTML encode and decode, and line-ending conversion are also in the “Actions” menu.

These actions work on several selected entries at once, too. On Mac, the “Actions” menu offers the link cleanup and line-ending actions.

Working with links

If you copy a lot of web links, turn on the Links section. It shows only entries that are a single web address, so they are not mixed in with your other text.

  • Turn it on: in Preferences, check the option “Show Links history tab” (Windows: on the “General” tab). Then press Alt+L in the history window to go to Links (Mac: use the Command and number shown in the View menu). Alt+T goes back to Text, and Ctrl+Tab moves through all sections.
  • Readable labels: instead of reading out a long address, Clipman reads a short label made from the address, such as “Issues 50; github.com/OnjLouis/Clipman/issues/50”. The full address is still what gets copied.
  • Open a link in your browser: select it and press Alt+Enter (Mac: Option+Enter).
  • Use the page's title as the name: choose “Use website title as name” from the entry's context menu (press the Applications key or Shift+F10 on Windows). Clipman asks first, because this contacts the website once.
  • Remove tracking: many links carry extra tracking codes after a question mark. “Remove URL tracking” strips them. “Clean link for sharing” also removes things like a YouTube start time, so the person you send it to starts from the beginning.

Keeping formatting and images

Normally Clipman stores plain text. If you want to keep formatting, such as headings, bold text and links copied from a web page or a Word document, turn on Rich Text history.

  1. In Preferences, check the option “Preserve copied formatting and show rich text history” (Windows: on the “General” tab).
  2. Copy something with formatting. It appears in the new Rich Text section (Windows: Alt+R) as well as in Text history.
  3. Copy it back from Rich Text history and paste it into a word processor. The formatting comes with it.

Only things you copy after turning this on keep their formatting. Editing the text in Entry Properties removes the formatting, because it would no longer match the text.

Images: a further option, “Include copied images in rich text history”, lets Clipman store PNG and JPEG pictures. This is mainly useful for passing photos between your phone and computer through shared history. On a computer, copying an image entry and pasting it into File Explorer or Finder saves it as an image file. On iPhone or Android, share a photo to Clipman from the Photos app. Pressing Alt+Enter (Mac: Option+Enter) on an image entry opens it in your default image viewer.

File History

When you copy or cut files and folders in File Explorer or Finder, Clipman notes it in File History. It stores where the files are, not the files themselves, and these records stay on the computer that made them.

Worked example: earlier you copied a document to paste into another folder, then copied lots of text since. You want to paste the document again.

  1. Open the history window and press Alt+I to go to File History (Mac: use the Command and number shown in the View menu). Each row starts with the file or folder name, then says whether it was a copy or a cut, and how many files.
  2. Press Alt+Delete (Mac: Option+Backspace). This clears out events whose files are no longer there, for example because they were moved or deleted since. It does not delete any actual files. It is a good habit, because it means everything left in the list can still be pasted.
  3. Select the event you want and press Enter. The files are put back on the clipboard and the window closes.
  4. In File Explorer or Finder, go to the destination folder and paste.

Other things you can do in File History:

  • Ctrl+C (Mac: Command+C) copies the file paths as text.
  • Ctrl+Enter (Mac: Command+Enter) opens the folder containing the file, with the file selected.
  • Ctrl+Delete (Mac: Control+Backspace) clears all unpinned events. Again, no actual files are deleted.
  • If you would like Clipman to clear out unavailable events for you, check the option “Automatically remove unavailable file-history events” on the “File history” tab of Preferences.
  • Pinning, moving and the Ctrl+1 to Ctrl+0 shortcuts work just as they do in Text history.

Other useful features

ClipMerge: build one item from several copies

ClipMerge is off by default. Turn it on with the option “Turn on ClipMerge” on the “General” tab of Preferences.

Worked example: you are reading a list of contacts in a file and want to collect three phone numbers into one clipboard item that goes into Clipman's text history.

  1. Select the first phone number and copy it once, as normal.
  2. Select the second phone number and press the copy shortcut twice quickly, within half a second. You hear the merge sound. The clipboard now holds both numbers, one per line.
  3. Select the third number and press copy twice quickly again.
  4. Paste. All three numbers arrive together. The combined item is also saved in your Text history.

The time allowed between the two presses, and what goes between the merged pieces, can be changed on the same tab. ClipMerge only merges copies from the same app, so it will not accidentally combine unrelated things.

ClipMerge and selecting several entries reach the same result in different ways. ClipMerge builds the combined item as you copy. Selecting several entries combines them afterwards, from history. Use whichever fits the way you work.

Push an entry to your other devices

If you share history between devices (see Part 5), select an entry and press Ctrl+P (Mac: Command+P) to send it to your other devices as if you had just copied it. Devices that have the remote clipboard option turned on place it on their clipboard. This works whether you share history through a shared folder or through Clipman Server, so you do not need a server for it. See Getting text from one device onto another's clipboard.

Save the clipboard once while monitoring is off

The tray menu's “Save current clipboard to history” command, or a shortcut you set on the “Hotkeys” tab, saves whatever is on the clipboard right now, even if monitoring is off. This could allow for using Clipman more selectively, if that's your preference: leave monitoring off, and save only the things you choose.

Open history in the right section

The option “Open history to the most recent clipboard type”, on the “General” tab, makes the history window open on Links after you copy a link, on File History after you copy a file, and so on.

Rearrange the sections

Focus a section tab and press Alt+Left Arrow or Alt+Right Arrow (Mac: Option+Left Arrow or Option+Right Arrow) to move it. On Windows and Linux, Alt+T, Alt+L, Alt+R and Alt+I keep working wherever the tabs are.

Import, export and backups

Use Ctrl+E (Mac: Command+E) to export your history to a file, and Ctrl+I (Mac: Command+I) to import one. Choose the .clipdb format for backups, because it can be password protected. JSON and text exports are readable by anyone who opens the file. On Windows, Clipman can also import history from the older Clipman and from Ditto.

Send a text file to Clipman (Windows)

In Preferences, check the option “Add Clipman to the Windows Send To menu for text files” on the “Startup and updates” tab. Then, in File Explorer, open the context menu on a text file, choose “Send to”, and choose “Clipman - add text to history”. The file's contents become a new entry.

Part 4: Privacy and security

Clipman records what you copy, and some of what you copy is private. This part shows how to stop Clipman recording things it should not, and how to protect what it does record. You can use as many or as few of these as you like.

Pausing Clipman for a moment

The simplest protection is to turn monitoring off before you copy something private, and back on afterwards.

  • Windows and Linux: Ctrl+Alt+Grave
  • Mac: Option+Shift+Backslash

You hear the “off” sound, and while monitoring is off you hear the “skip” sound each time you copy, to remind you nothing is being saved. Press the shortcut again to turn monitoring back on. Clipman remembers whether monitoring was on or off, even after a restart, so listen for the skip sound if you are not sure.

Tutorial 12: Stop Clipman recording a password manager

What you will learn: how to make Clipman ignore everything copied from one app.

New terms: an ignored application is an app whose copies Clipman never saves.

Many password managers already mark their copies as private, and Clipman on Windows respects that automatically. If yours does not, or you want to be certain, add it to the ignored list.

Worked example (Windows):

  1. Make sure your password manager is running.
  2. Open Preferences and press Ctrl+4 for the “Storage and Password” tab.
  3. Activate the “Add running app” button (Alt+I). A list of running programs opens.
  4. Find your password manager in the list, for example KeePassXC or 1Password, and choose it. It is added to the “Ignored applications” box.
  5. Close Preferences with Escape.
  6. Test it: copy something from your password manager. You hear no copy sound and nothing new appears in history.

On Mac: type the app's name, such as KeePassXC, into the ignored applications box in Preferences, one app per line, then activate the “Save and Close” button.

Sensitive data exclusions

Sometimes private things are copied from ordinary apps, such as a card number from a web page. Sensitive data exclusions let Clipman recognize the shape of some kinds of private text and refuse to save it. Your clipboard is not affected, so you can still paste it once.

  1. Open Preferences and press Ctrl+6 for the “Sensitive data” tab.
  2. Set “Sensitive data mode” to “Exclude from history”.
  3. In the “Exclusion presets” list, check the kinds of text you want excluded. The choices are credit card numbers, US Social Security numbers, international phone numbers, long API keys or tokens, software license keys, and an approximate pattern for US driver's licenses.
  4. Close Preferences.

None of these are checked to begin with, because pattern matching can catch harmless text by mistake. For example, “international phone numbers” also catches your own phone number when you copy it. Check only the ones that suit you. The full manual has harmless test values you can copy to check each one is working. You will hear the exclude sound when one is caught.

Protecting history with a password

Clipman's history file is compressed, so it cannot be read by simply opening it, but anyone with Clipman could load it. A history password encrypts the file so it is unreadable without the password. A password is required for Secrets, and when sharing history through a shared folder with a phone or through Clipman Server.

To set a history password on Windows:

  1. Open Preferences and press Ctrl+4 for the “Storage and Password” tab.
  2. Type your password in the “History password” field and again in the “Confirm password” field. Check the option “Show history password” if you want to hear what you typed. Or activate the “Generate password” button to have Clipman create a strong one and copy it to your clipboard. Clipman deliberately does not save that copy in history.
  3. Decide about the option “Remember history password on this computer”, explained below.
  4. Close Preferences.

The option “Remember history password on this computer”:

  • Checked: Clipman opens without asking for the password. This is convenient, especially if Clipman starts automatically. The password is protected by your Windows account or your Mac Keychain.
  • Unchecked: Clipman asks for the password each time it starts and forgets it when it closes. This is safer if other people use your computer account.

Important: keep the password somewhere safe, such as your password manager. If you lose it, nobody can recover your history, including the developer.

Leaving both password fields blank keeps the current password. To remove a password completely, use the “Use no password” button.

Tutorial 13: Store a private snippet in Secrets

What you will learn: how to keep a short private item, such as a work PIN or the password for a program that has no password manager support, where only you can paste it.

New terms: Secrets is a separate list, protected by your history password. Secrets are typed in by you, never captured from the clipboard, never shown in normal history, and never shared with your other devices.

Before you start: you need a history password. See Protecting history with a password.

Worked example (Windows): you store your door entry code and give it the shortcut Ctrl+Win+F12.

  1. Press Ctrl+Shift+E with the history window open, or choose “Secrets” from the tray menu. Mac: Command+Shift+E, or “Secrets” in the menu extra.
  2. Enter your history password when asked.
  3. Press Insert to add a secret. You may need to use your screen reader's bypass key before pressing Insert, or simply tab to the “Add” button. The Add Secret window opens.
  4. In the “Name” field, type Door code.
  5. In the “Secret” field, type the code. In the “Confirm” field, type it again. Check the option “Show secret” if you want to hear it.
  6. In the “Quick Paste hotkey” field, press Ctrl+Win+F12. This is optional.
  7. Activate the “OK” button, then press Escape to close Secrets.

Now pressing Ctrl+Win+F12 in any app pastes your door code, then restores whatever was on your clipboard before.

In the Secrets list, Enter pastes the selected secret straight away, F2 edits it, and Delete removes it. Each of these also has a button you can tab to. The list only ever shows names and shortcuts, never the secrets themselves.

A word of caution: Secrets is a convenience, not a replacement for a password manager. For website passwords, a proper password manager is safer.

Part 5: Using Clipman on more than one device

Clipman can keep the same history on your computer, phone and tablet. Copy an address on your laptop and it is waiting in Clipman on your iPhone. This is called syncing, and it is where Clipman saves you from emailing things to yourself.

Choosing how to share history

There are three ways to store history. Pick the one that matches the devices you use.

  • Local: history stays on one device. This is how Clipman starts, and it is all you need if you only use one device.
  • Shared folder: history lives in a folder that a service you already use, such as Dropbox, OneDrive, iCloud Drive or a home network share, keeps in step between your devices. This is the easiest way to sync, and it works on Windows, Mac, iPhone, iPad and Android. There is nothing extra to install, but how quickly changes appear depends on that service.
  • Clipman Server: you run a small separate program on a computer that is always on, such as a home PC, a Raspberry Pi or a rented server. It is faster and more predictable than a shared folder, and it is the only option on Linux, but it takes more setting up.

If you are unsure, start with a shared folder using a service you already have. You can move to Clipman Server later. This works well enough if you're sharing history between two Windows computers.

Before you start

  • Back up first. On each computer that already has history you care about, export it with Ctrl+E (Mac: Command+E) as a .clipdb file, and keep the file somewhere outside the shared folder.
  • Choose a history password. Phones and Clipman Server require one, and every device must use exactly the same password. Store it in your password manager. See Protecting history with a password.
  • Update every device. Install the latest Clipman on every device you plan to connect.
  • Use a dedicated folder. Make a new, empty folder just for Clipman, such as Clipman Shared in your cloud storage service. Do not edit, rename or delete the files Clipman puts there.

Tutorial 14: Share history between a Windows PC and an iPhone using Dropbox

What you will learn: how to set up a shared folder from start to finish, using one of the most common combinations. The same steps work with an iPad, and the Windows half works for a second Windows PC too.

You will need:

  • the Dropbox app installed and signed in on both the PC and the iPhone, using the same Dropbox account;
  • Clipman on both;
  • a history password you have chosen and saved somewhere safe.

Step 1: Create the shared folder on the PC

  1. Open File Explorer and go to your Dropbox folder. It is usually C:\Users\YourName\Dropbox.
  2. Create a new folder with Ctrl+Shift+N, name it Clipman Shared, and press Enter.
  3. Make sure Dropbox keeps this folder on your PC rather than only online. Open the folder's context menu with the Applications key and choose Dropbox's “Make available offline” option if it is offered. On Windows 11 you may first need to choose “Show more options”.

Step 2: Back up your existing history

If Clipman on this PC already has entries you want to keep, open the history window, press Ctrl+E, choose the .clipdb format, and save the file to your Documents folder, not the shared folder.

Step 3: Point Clipman on the PC at the shared folder

  1. Open Preferences and press Ctrl+4 for the “Storage and Password” tab.
  2. Check that “Storage type” is set to “Local or shared folder”.
  3. Go to the “Data folder” field (Alt+D). Either type the full path of your new folder, such as C:\Users\YourName\Dropbox\Clipman Shared, or activate the “Browse” button and choose it. Choose the folder itself, not a file inside it.
  4. Type your history password in the “History password” field and again in the “Confirm password” field.
  5. Check the option “Remember history password on this computer” if you want Clipman to start without asking.
  6. Close Preferences with Escape. Your changes are saved as the window closes.

Clipman now keeps this PC's history in the Dropbox folder. A few files appear there, including clipman-history.clipdb and a settings file named after your computer. Leave them alone.

Open the history window. If your earlier entries are missing, press Ctrl+I and import the backup you made in Step 2. They are added to the shared history.

Before going on, give Dropbox a minute to upload the new files.

Step 4: Make sure the iPhone can see Dropbox in the Files app

  1. Open the Files app on your iPhone and go to the “Browse” tab.
  2. Look under Locations for “Dropbox”. If it is there, open it and check you can find the Clipman Shared folder.
  3. If Dropbox is not listed, activate the “More” button on the Browse screen, choose “Edit”, turn on Dropbox, and choose “Done”.

Step 5: Connect Clipman on the iPhone

  1. Open Clipman, activate the “More” button at the top right, and choose “Settings”.
  2. Under “History storage”, set “Storage mode” to “Shared folder”. A Shared folder sync section appears.
  3. In the “Shared history password” field, type exactly the same password you used on the PC.
  4. Activate the “Choose shared folder” button. A folder picker opens. Go to Dropbox, then Clipman Shared, and choose “Open” to select that folder. Select the folder itself, not a file inside it.
  5. Check that the line above the button now reads “Shared folder: Clipman Shared”.
  6. Activate the “Save” button at the top of Settings.
  7. Stay in Clipman for a moment. When the status line at the bottom says “Ready. Shared folder sync connected.”, you are done. The first sync can take a little while.

Step 6: Test it

  1. On the PC, copy a short phrase, such as Hello from my PC. Wait a few seconds for Dropbox to upload it.
  2. On the iPhone, open the Files app, go to Dropbox, and open the Clipman Shared folder. This prompts Dropbox to fetch the latest version of the files.
  3. Open Clipman. “Hello from my PC” appears near the top of the list.
  4. Now go the other way. In Clipman on the iPhone, activate the “Quick Clip” button. Swipe right past the “Name” and “Group” fields and the two switches until you reach the “Clipboard text” field. Double-tap it if the keyboard is not already showing, and type Hello from my iPhone. Then activate the “Save” button at the top.
  5. Open the history window on the PC. Your iPhone entry appears there too, once Dropbox has passed it along.

Things to know about Dropbox on iPhone

  • Visiting the folder in the Files app is essential. Dropbox on iPhone is slow to tell other apps about new files. In testing for this guide, new entries from the PC only appeared in Clipman after browsing to the Clipman Shared folder in the Files app first. Make it a habit: Files app, Dropbox, Clipman Shared, then Clipman.
  • Clipman only syncs while it is open on the iPhone. iOS does not let it work in the background, so open Clipman and give it a moment when you want the latest entries.
  • Each device keeps its own copy. If Dropbox or your connection is down, Clipman keeps working with its own copy and catches up later. It merges changes rather than overwriting one device with another, so nothing is lost while you wait.
  • If Dropbox is too slow for you, iCloud Drive or Clipman Server may suit you better. Both are described below.

Other shared folders: OneDrive, iCloud Drive, Google Drive and network shares

The steps are the same as in Tutorial 14. Only the folder changes.

  • Several Windows PCs, or Windows and Mac: follow Step 3 on each computer, choosing the same shared folder and the same password. On Mac, the storage settings are in Preferences.
  • OneDrive or Google Drive on a computer: make sure the folder is set to stay on your computer, using the provider's “Always keep on this device” or “Available offline” option.
  • iCloud Drive with Windows: install iCloud for Windows from the Microsoft Store, sign in, turn on iCloud Drive, and set your Clipman folder to “Always keep on this device”. Apple's iCloud Drive setup guide for Windows has the details. iCloud Drive is usually the most dependable choice between iPhone and other devices.
  • On iPhone and iPad, not every provider works. Clipman needs to keep access to a whole folder. iCloud Drive and Dropbox allow this. At the time of writing, some other providers, reportedly including Google Drive and OneDrive, do not let iPhone apps choose a folder at all. If you cannot select your folder, use iCloud Drive or Dropbox, or Clipman Server.
  • Android: in Clipman's Settings, choose “Shared Folder”, pick the folder with Android's folder picker, enter the same password, and activate the “Save” button.
  • Network shares: on Windows and Mac, a shared folder on another computer or a home storage device works if it appears as an ordinary folder or mapped drive.

Clipman Server, in brief

Clipman Server is a separate program that holds your shared history on a computer that is always on. It never sees your password, so your history stays encrypted on the server. It is worth the extra effort if a shared folder is too slow, if you use Linux, or if you do not want to rely on a cloud service. It may also prove to be a good alternative for sharing history with an iPhone.

Setting up the server itself is covered in the Clipman Server manual. Once it is running, it creates a connection file ending in .clpconf, which holds the server's address and access token. Connecting a device then only takes a minute:

  • Windows: open Preferences, press Ctrl+4, set “Storage type” to “Clipman Server”, activate the “Import server file” button, choose the .clpconf file and confirm, enter your history password in both password fields, and close Preferences.
  • Mac: open Preferences, choose Clipman Server storage, activate the “Import Server File” button, enter your history password, and activate the “Save and Close” button.
  • iPhone, iPad and Android: open the .clpconf file from the Files app or your file manager, or use the “Import server connection file” button in Clipman's Settings. Check the address, enter your history password, and activate the “Save” button.

The connection file works like a key to your server. Send it to your devices in a private way, and delete it once every device is set up.

Sync rules and channels, in brief

Sync rules are an advanced, optional feature for people with a large shared history. They split history into named parts called channels, decided by group, by device, or by whether an entry contains an image. Each device then chooses which channels to download, which saves data and time. Everything not claimed by a channel stays in the main part, called core, which every device always gets.

Most people never need sync rules. They currently work only with Clipman Server, and channels are created on Windows, Mac, Linux or the command line. Phones and tablets can choose which channels they download. Channels organize history. They do not hide anything, since every device with the same password can read every channel. See the full manual's Sync Rules and Channels section if you want to use them.

Getting text from one device onto another's clipboard

Syncing puts new entries into history on your other devices, but it does not automatically put them on their clipboards. If you want that too, there is an option for it. It works with a shared folder as well as with Clipman Server.

Worked example: you copy a link on your iPhone and want to paste it straight away on your PC.

  1. On the PC, open Preferences and, on the “General” tab, check the option “Put new text received or sent to Clipman on the clipboard”. Close Preferences.
  2. On the iPhone, copy the link in Safari, open Clipman, and activate the “Paste” button to add it.
  3. Once it syncs, the PC plays the remote sound. The link is on the PC's clipboard, ready to paste with Ctrl+V.

The equivalent settings are “Copy latest remote item to iOS clipboard” on iPhone and iPad and “Copy remote additions to Android clipboard” on Android. On mobile they only work while Clipman is open. To send an older entry this way, select it and use Push to other devices (Ctrl+P, Mac: Command+P).

Part 6: Clipman on iPhone, iPad and Android

How the mobile apps differ

The biggest difference is that phones and tablets do not let apps watch the clipboard in the background. On a computer, Clipman saves everything you copy. On a phone, Clipman only knows about something when you give it to Clipman. You can do that in several ways:

  • open Clipman and use the “Paste” button;
  • turn on the option “Add current clipboard on launch” in Settings, so Clipman offers to add whatever you copied each time you open it;
  • use the Share button in another app and choose Clipman;
  • use Quick Clip to type something new;
  • on iPhone and iPad, use Siri, Shortcuts or the Action Button.

The mobile apps also only sync while they are open, and they have no File History or Secrets.

Finding your way around on iPhone and iPad

The main screen

  • At the top right are three buttons: “Paste”, which adds what you last copied; “Quick Clip”, which opens an editor for a new entry; and “More”, which holds the other history sections and Settings.
  • The screen also has a search field, a “Filter” button for groups and devices, and the list of entries. Each entry is a single item, so one swipe moves to the next entry.
  • At the bottom is a status line showing the number of entries and whether syncing is working. Double-tapping it moves to the bottom of the list.

Adding what you copied

Worked example: save a phone number from a web page.

  1. In Safari, select the phone number and copy it.
  2. Open Clipman and double-tap the “Paste” button. A two-finger double-tap, the VoiceOver Magic Tap gesture, does the same thing from anywhere in the list.
  3. The number is added at the top of the list and you hear the copy sound.

If iOS asks whether to allow pasting, choose “Allow”. To stop being asked, see the iPhone setup steps.

Typing a new entry with Quick Clip

  1. Double-tap the “Quick Clip” button. A form opens with “Cancel” and “Save” buttons at the top.
  2. The form has the “Name” and “Group” fields and the “Pinned” and “Template” switches first, then the “Clipboard text” field at the bottom. The text you type in “Clipboard text” is what becomes the entry.
  3. Swipe right until you reach the “Clipboard text” field. If the keyboard is not already showing, double-tap the field to start typing. Fill in Name and Group too if you like.
  4. Double-tap the “Save” button at the top. It stays dimmed until there is some text in the “Clipboard text” field.

If you leave Clipman before saving, your unfinished Quick Clip is kept and waiting for you when you come back.

Using an entry

  • Copy it: double-tap the entry. It goes on the iOS clipboard, ready to paste in another app.
  • Other actions: swipe up or down with the VoiceOver Actions rotor to find Open (for links), View, Edit, Pin or Unpin, and Delete.
  • Move through the list: a three-finger swipe up or down moves a page at a time.
  • Change section: a three-finger swipe left or right moves between Text, Links and Rich Text, if they are turned on. The “More” button also lists them.

Settings

Open Settings from the “More” button. Changes only take effect when you activate the “Save” button at the top. The “Cancel” button, or the VoiceOver scrub gesture, discards them. Useful settings include:

  • “Add current clipboard on launch”: offers to add whatever you copied each time you open Clipman.
  • “Require biometric or device authentication”: asks for Face ID, Touch ID or your passcode every time you return to Clipman.
  • “Enable links history” and “Preserve copied formatting and show Rich Text history”: the same optional sections as on a computer.
  • “Sort normal entries”: Manual order, Newest first, Oldest first, or Text.
  • “Confirm before deleting entries”, “Play sounds” and “Use haptics”.
  • “Open Manual”, under Help, opens the full manual in your browser.

Shortcuts from the Home Screen and Siri

Focus the Clipman icon on the Home Screen and double-tap and hold to see three quick actions: “Quick Clip”, “Add Clipboard to Clipman” and “Copy Latest Clip”. The same three are available to Siri, for example “Add clipboard to Clipman” or “Copy latest from Clipman”, and in the Shortcuts app.

Tutorial 15: Copy your latest clip with the Action Button

What you will learn: how to get the newest entry from your synced history onto your iPhone's clipboard with one button press. This needs an iPhone with an Action Button, and works best once you have set up syncing as in Part 5.

  1. Open the iOS Settings app and choose “Action Button”.
  2. Swipe through the choices until you reach “Shortcut”.
  3. Activate “Choose a Shortcut”, find Clipman, and choose “Copy Latest Clip”.
  4. Leave Settings.

Try it: copy some text on your computer. On your iPhone, press and hold the Action Button. Clipman opens, checks your shared history, and puts the newest entry on the iPhone's clipboard. Paste it wherever you need it. If Clipman cannot reach your shared history, it tells you, rather than copying an out-of-date entry. If you use Dropbox, remember the Files app step in Tutorial 14 first.

You could assign “Add Clipboard to Clipman” instead, to save what you have just copied on the iPhone with one press.

Finding your way around on Android

Clipman for Android works much like the iPhone version, with TalkBack.

  • Toolbar: “Paste” adds what you last copied, “Quick Clip” creates a new entry, and “More” holds other sections, Settings and Close. There are also “Filter” and “Top” buttons.
  • Copy an entry: double-tap it.
  • Other actions: use TalkBack's actions menu for View, Edit, Pin or Unpin, Delete, and Open Link. Long-pressing an entry opens View Entry.
  • Status: the status at the bottom shows the number of entries and the sync state. Double-tapping it moves to the end of the list. Long-pressing it changes the sort order.
  • Share to Clipman: choose Clipman from another app's Share menu to add text, a link or a photo.
  • Settings: open from “More”. Activate the “Save” button at the top to keep changes. The “Cancel” button, or Android's Back, discards them.
  • Updates: Clipman checks for updates itself. Settings has a “Check Now” button. Android always asks you to approve installing an update.

Troubleshooting

The history shortcut does nothing

  • Check Clipman is running. Look for it in the notification area (Windows) or menu bar (Mac). If it is not there, start it again.
  • Your keyboard layout may be the cause. The default shortcuts use the Backslash and Grave keys, which sit in different places on non-US keyboards. On many layouts, including the UK one, Ctrl+Alt also acts as AltGr. Choose shortcuts that suit your keyboard: open Preferences from the tray menu, press Ctrl+3 for the “Hotkeys” tab, focus the “Show history hotkey” field, and press the new combination, such as Ctrl+Win+F11. Do the same for the “Toggle on/off hotkey” field.
  • Another program may already use it. Screen readers, screen reader add-ons such as Leasey, and other utilities reserve many shortcuts. Try a different combination.
  • You can always open history from the tray menu with “Show or hide history”.

Things I copy are not being saved

  • Monitoring may be off. If you hear the skip sound when you copy, press the monitoring shortcut to turn it back on. The status bar in the history window also says “Monitoring off”.
  • The app may be ignored. Check the “Ignored applications” box on the “Storage and Password” tab.
  • The text may look private. If you hear the exclude sound, a sensitive data preset or a privacy marker from the source app has stopped it. This is deliberate.
  • It may be a file. Files and folders go to File History (Alt+I), not Text history.
  • On a phone, nothing is saved automatically. See How the mobile apps differ.

Pressing Enter in history does not paste

That is normal. Enter puts the entry on the clipboard, and you then paste it yourself. To have Clipman paste for you, turn on the option “After Enter, paste into the previous application” on the “General” tab. On Mac, also allow Clipman to control your computer when macOS asks, then restart Clipman.

Only one entry was pasted when I selected several

Moving with an arrow key without holding Ctrl selects only the entry you land on, and clears the rest of the selection. Go back through Tutorial 2, and keep Ctrl held down while you move between the entries you want.

Clipman says storage is unavailable

Clipman cannot reach its data folder or server. This usually happens when a cloud service is not running, a network drive is disconnected, or the computer is offline. Clipman keeps running, and nothing is lost. Fix the connection, then choose “Retry storage” from the tray menu on Windows, or “Retry Storage” from the menu extra on Mac.

My phone is not showing entries from my computer

  • Keep Clipman open on the phone for a few seconds. It only syncs while open.
  • Check the status line says the sync is connected.
  • Check every device uses exactly the same folder and exactly the same history password. A different password does not show an error. It simply opens a different, separate history.
  • On the computer, check that your cloud service has finished uploading.
  • With Dropbox on iPhone, open the Files app, go to the shared folder, then return to Clipman. In testing, this step was needed every time.

On iPhone, Quick Clip does not let me type

The “Clipboard text” field is at the bottom of the Quick Clip form, after the Name and Group fields and the two switches, and the keyboard does not always appear by itself. Swipe right until you reach “Clipboard text”, then double-tap it to start typing. See Typing a new entry with Quick Clip.

Clipman asks for my password every time it starts

Check the option “Remember history password on this computer” on the “Storage and Password” tab, enter the password when asked, and close Preferences.

I have forgotten my history password

Encrypted history cannot be opened without its password, and nobody can recover it for you. If you have an older backup you can open, import it. Otherwise, point Clipman at a new, empty data folder and start again with a new password.

Where are Clipman's files?

On Windows, press Ctrl+Shift+O in the history window, or choose “Open settings folder” from the tray menu. On Mac, use “Open Settings Folder” in the menu extra.

Reporting a problem

Open diagnostics with Alt+F1 (Mac: Option+F1). It describes how Clipman is set up and what it last did. Include it when you report a problem through the Clipman issues page or the “Contact” command in the Help menu.
